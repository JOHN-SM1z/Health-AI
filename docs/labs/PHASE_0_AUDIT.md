# Laboratory Module — Phase 0 Architectural Audit

Status: **audit only**. This phase changes no schema, no application code, no
UI and no data. Every statement below is grounded in the repository as of
2026-10-05 (branch `claude/sharp-ptolemy-rl1rnc`, 47 migrations). Paths are
relative to the repository root; migrations are under `supabase/migrations/`.

Explicitly **deferred**: critical lab-result alerts (deferred until validated
with doctors and clinical managers). Phase 0–21 designs must not assume them.

## Owner decisions (2026-10-05) — resolve P1–P3, U5-adjacent, U6, U8, U9

1. **Access is "minimum necessary by role and purpose"**, replacing the blanket
   "no clinical text" rule for laboratory data. `AGENTS.md` is updated:
   - Lab staff see lab orders and the lab data needed to perform the test and
     enter results — not unrelated doctor notes, diagnoses, prescriptions,
     referrals or history.
   - Patients see only their own **finalized** results, through verified
     Telegram identity.
   - AI may later process only structured lab-result data for the approved
     summary feature — not notes/history, not unnecessary identifiers.
   - Doctor-authored clinical text (`referrals`, `clinical_records`) keeps its
     existing doctor-only rule unchanged.
2. **Lab ordering has no role restriction.** Any authenticated staff member of
   the clinic (owner, manager, admin, receptionist, doctor, lab staff) may
   order any active test for a patient of that clinic. No per-role or per-test
   ordering permissions. Requirements are only: authenticated, member of the
   clinic, valid patient/order data. Three entry paths are supported: doctor
   consultation, walk-in (reception/lab), and external result import.
   Ordering does not by itself grant access to result values.
3. **Patients gain `date_of_birth` and `sex`.** DOB is strongly preferred and
   required for patients entering the lab workflow; `sex` is a clinical
   attribute for reference-range selection and may be unknown/null — staff
   must not be forced to guess. Identity capture at reception: ID/passport,
   name, DOB, phone, sex.

These decisions supersede §6 P1–P3 and §9 U6, U8 and U9 below.

## 2. Answers to the audit questions

### Q1. Does a laboratory / test / result model exist?

**No laboratory domain exists.** The only lab concept is two values of the
free-text clinical record type:

- `clinical_record_type` gained `lab_order` (`20260928000001_clinical_handoff_types.sql:22`); `lab_result` existed from `20260927000005_clinical_records.sql:31-37`.
- A lab record is just `summary` (≤300 chars) + optional `details` (≤4000) + optional `code` on `clinical_records`, written by the doctor in their own consultation.
- UI: labels in `src/lib/clinical-records/categories.ts:27-28,52-53,64-65` ("Tahlilga yo‘llanma", "Tahlil natijasi"); the chart summary groups them via `SUMMARY_TYPES` (`src/app/doctor/patients/[id]/page.tsx:129-135`).
- `TASKS.md` (Someday): "Lab integration – lab results are typed in by doctors; no laboratory system feeds them".

There are no test catalog, parameter, reference range, sample, structured
value, result file or lab status anywhere in the schema or `src/`.

### Q2. Does `specialties` represent clinic departments; can it be reused for lab departments?

`specialties` (`20260813000003_catalog.sql:3-12`: `id, clinic_id, name,
description, sort_order, active`, unique `(clinic_id, name)`) is a
clinic-scoped **medical specialty / bookable direction** ("Yo‘nalishlar" in
the admin nav). It is wired into:

- the **public** catalog `/api/catalog` (returned to unauthenticated Mini App users, `src/app/api/catalog/route.ts:23-36`);
- `doctors.specialty_id` (one specialty per doctor) and `services.specialty_id`;
- **AI navigation**, which suggests a specialty to patients (`src/lib/ai/navigation.ts:29-44`).

Reusing it for laboratory sections (e.g. "Biochemistry", "Haematology")
would put lab sections into patient booking and into AI routing — a
behavioural change to two safety-relevant surfaces, and a different concept
(a bench/section of the lab, not a medical direction a patient books).

### Q3. Is a new LabDepartment entity necessary?

**Not as a department entity.** The specification needs a *grouping* of
tests for configuration and dashboards, not an organisational unit with
staff, schedules or bookings. Recommendation: a lightweight, clinic-configured
**lab test category** (name, sort order, active) owned by the lab catalog —
not a `LabDepartment`, and not a reuse of `specialties`. To be confirmed in
Phase 1 (see §9, U1).

### Q4. Can `clinical_records` represent lab orders/results, or is a dedicated lab domain required?

**A dedicated lab domain is required.** `clinical_records` is deliberately
narrow and cannot carry the lab workflow:

| Lab requirement | `clinical_records` today |
|---|---|
| Lab staff enter results | Author must be an **active doctor holding the `doctor` role**, and `created_by` must be that doctor's profile (`clinical_records_validate()`, `20260927000005:92-162`) |
| Results arrive after the visit | Record must sit in the **author's own** appointment (`clinical_records_consultation_fkey`, `20260927000005:62-65`) which is `in_progress` or `completed` |
| Structured values, units, ranges | Text only (`summary`, `details`, `code`) |
| Order / sample / result lifecycle | No status column; rows are insert-only |
| Versioned corrections with verification | At most **one** correction per record (`clinical_records_one_correction`), same author only |
| Attachments | None |

What *should* be reused from it: the provenance pattern (composite FK to
the ordering doctor's own appointment), DB-clock timestamps, insert-only
immutability with explicit corrections, `creation_key` idempotency, and
id-only audit triggers. `clinical_records` remains the place for the
**doctor's own interpretation** of a lab result (an `assessment` /
`consultation_note` in their consultation).

Open: what happens to the legacy free-text `lab_order` / `lab_result` record
types once structured lab data exists (see U2) — they must not become a
second source of truth.

### Q5. How are payments represented; can they safely support lab orders?

- `payments` (`20260813000005_appointments_payments.sql:33-50`): `appointment_id uuid NOT NULL`, **`unique (appointment_id)`**, `amount numeric(12,2)`, `currency 'UZS'`, `status payment_status`, `provider payment_provider`, `provider_reference`, `payment_url`, `paid_at`, `paid_by`, `metadata`. Composite FKs to appointment and patient, both `on delete cascade` (`20260930000006:96-102`).
- Enums: `payment_status` = `unpaid, pending, paid, failed, refunded, manual_review`; `payment_provider` = `manual, click, payme, cash, card_terminal`.
- Server-controlled: authenticated sessions cannot write (`payments_block_direct_write`, `20260822000001_payments_server_managed.sql`; grants revoked in `20260930000002`). Legal transitions and audit live in `src/lib/payments/status.ts` (`canTransition`, `transitionPaymentStatus`, line 19/28).
- Amount is resolved in the database inside `book_appointment` as `coalesce(doctor_services.price_override, services.price)`, never from the browser.
- Providers: `src/lib/payments/provider.ts` (`ManualPaymentProvider`, `ClickPaymentProvider` with signature check + idempotent webhook in `click-webhook.ts`, `PaymePaymentProvider` stub).

**The payment model is strictly one payment per appointment.** A lab order
cannot get its own payment row today, and attaching it to the consultation's
payment is impossible (the slot is already taken by the consultation fee).

Gaps that the product prompts assume exist but **do not**:

- **No "Kassa" / cashier screen.** The manual payment route `POST /api/admin/appointments/[id]/payment` (owner/admin) exists but **no UI calls it**; admin pages only render status badges.
- **No receipt / digital-receipt system.** Nothing generates receipts. Prompt 6's "reuse the existing digital-receipt architecture" has nothing to reuse.
- **Refund is a status only** (`paid → refunded`); no refund amount, no partial refunds.
- Analytics revenue (`src/lib/analytics/aggregate.ts`) counts completed appointments with a paid payment. Separately, `src/app/api/admin/dashboard/route.ts:54` computes today's revenue from `services.price`, not `payments.amount` — an existing inconsistency to resolve before any lab revenue dashboard (Prompt 17 requires authoritative payment records).

Smallest safe extension (proposal, to be designed in Phase 1/6): generalise
the payment *subject* rather than adding a second engine — make
`appointment_id` nullable, add a nullable unique `lab_order_id` (composite FK
with `clinic_id`), and a CHECK that exactly one subject is set. Everything
else (status machine, provider layer, direct-write block, audit trigger,
Click webhook amount check) is reused unchanged. Every place that assumes
`payments.appointment_id` is non-null (booking engine, `/api/me/appointments`,
analytics, Click webhook lookup) must be reviewed in that phase.

### Q6. How are files/PDFs stored and accessed?

- One storage bucket exists: **`voice-messages`**, private (`20260813000011_storage.sql:6-8`), path `<clinic_id>/<voice_message_id>.<ext>`.
- Policies: service role full access; staff read limited to own clinic folder and to owner/admin/manager/receptionist (`20260927000004:31-45`); the staff upload policy was dropped, so uploads are server-only (`20260930000006:161`).
- Retention exists only for voice (`src/lib/voice/retention.ts`, `purgeExpiredVoiceMessages`).
- **No** document bucket, **no** upload/download API routes, **no** signed URL usage (`createSignedUrl` appears nowhere), no multipart handling, no PDF generation.

Lab documents therefore need a new **private** bucket following the voice
conventions (`<clinic_id>/…` prefix, server-only writes, no broad staff read
policy) plus server-issued short-lived signed URLs after authorization. This
is a new capability, but not a second architecture.

### Q7. Does a lab-specific role exist?

**No.** `staff_role` = `owner, manager, admin, receptionist, doctor`
(`20260813000001:53` + `20260818000023_role_based_authorization.sql:18-34`).

- `staff_roles` is **unique on `(clinic_id, profile_id)`** — one role per person per clinic. A doctor who is also a lab verifier, or a receptionist who also collects samples, cannot hold two roles.
- No permission map exists in TypeScript; authorization is role lists at each call site (`requireStaff`, `requireRoles`, `requireLinkedDoctor` in `src/lib/auth/guards.ts`; `ROLE_WEIGHT`, `hasRole`, `canViewPaymentDynamics` in `src/lib/auth/staff.ts`).
- `requireStaff(min)` is **weight-based** (owner 4, admin/manager 3, doctor 1, receptionist 0). A new lab role must be placed so weight comparisons never grant it admin/doctor surfaces by accident; exact-membership `requireRoles(...)` is the safer guard for lab routes.
- DB helpers: `is_clinic_staff(clinic, roles[])`, `current_doctor_id(clinic)`, `doctor_can_read_patient`, `doctor_can_read_appointment`.
- Adding an enum value requires its own migration (cannot be used in the same transaction); `build-full-db-setup.mjs` already inserts `commit;` after such files.

### Q8. How are notifications generated?

- `notification_jobs` (`20260813000008:4-24`) with `type notification_job_type` = `booking_confirmation, reminder_24h, reminder_2h, cancellation, reschedule, human_takeover`; unique `idempotency_key`.
- Enqueue: `src/lib/notifications/jobs.ts` (`enqueueNotificationJob` + appointment helpers).
- Atomic claim: `claim_due_notification_jobs(p_limit)` with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, service-role only (`20260813000014:41-69`, `20260912000001:40-41`).
- Worker: `POST /api/notifications/process` (CRON_SECRET, `src/lib/cron-auth.ts`), scheduled by `supabase/ops/scheduled-jobs.sql`.
- Processor (`src/lib/notifications/processor.ts`): templates are built from the **appointment context**; jobs without `appointment_id` are **skipped** ("job has no appointment", line ~201). Conversation-assigned-to-human suppression and never-resend-after-delivery are already handled.

Lab notifications need: new `notification_job_type` values (own migration), a
lab-order/result subject on the job, and a processor branch with
non-clinical templates ("Your laboratory result is ready").

### Q9. How is patient Telegram identity resolved?

- `validateTelegramInitDataForClinic()` (`src/lib/telegram/init-data.ts:83`) — HMAC check, 24 h max age, only the clinic's own bot tokens.
- `resolvePatientFromInitData(initData, clinicId)` → `getOrCreatePatient` keyed by `(clinic_id, telegram_user_id)` (`src/lib/patients/identity.ts:13,143`). Dev identity only when `devIdentityAllowed()`.
- `telegram_user_id` can only be set by the server (`patients_telegram_identity_server_only`, `20260813000021:58-82`).
- Patient-facing routes take `{initData}` in a POST body (`/api/me/appointments`, `/api/bookings`, …). `/start` has no deep-link payload routing (`src/lib/telegram/handlers.ts:243`); Mini App links are built by `bookingLink()` / `webAppUrl()`.

### Q10. How does doctor access to longitudinal clinical history work?

- DB decision: `doctor_patient_access(p_doctor_id, p_patient_id)` (final body `20260929000001:64-147`, SECURITY DEFINER, service-role only) → `own_patient`, `active_referral_ids`, `history_doctor_ids`, `referral_appointment_ids`.
- RLS backstop: `doctor_can_read_appointment(clinic, patient, doctor, appointment)` (final `20260929000001:152-171`).
- Server: `canDoctorAccessPatientClinicalData()` (`src/lib/clinical-access/access.ts:107`), fail-closed (503); `canSeeAppointment()` mirrors the RLS rule; denials audited via `patientAccessDenied()` (`denial.ts`).
- Read path: `getPatientWorkspace()` (`src/lib/clinical-access/workspace.ts:96-235`) with a **strict** audit `patient_clinical_record_viewed` (line ~208) before returning; `listVisibleClinicalRecords()` (`src/lib/clinical-records/service.ts:50-95`).
- `clinical_records` and `referrals` are **server-only**: `revoke all … from authenticated` (`20260929000001:216-217`).
- Visibility is **per appointment**, not per patient: a referred doctor sees the referring doctor's records only while the referral is accepted and unexpired. Lab orders/results should therefore inherit the visibility of the **ordering appointment** via the same functions — no new access model.

### Q11. How are audit events created?

- Table `audit_events` (`20260813000008:41-54` + `patient_id`, `referral_id` in `20260929000001:221-235`). Append-only: no INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE for anon/authenticated, no UPDATE/DELETE/TRUNCATE even for service role (`20260929000001:273-274`). `audit_events_guard` stamps time and checks tenant consistency (`20260930000006:324-353`).
- TS: `recordAudit` / `recordAudits` (`src/lib/audit.ts:71,76`), best-effort by default, `strict: true` → 503 when the audit cannot be written.
- DB triggers: generic `audit_track_changes()` (stores full row JSON — **must not** be attached to tables holding clinical text) on staff_roles, appointments, payments, time blocks, conversations; purpose-built **id-only** triggers for referrals and clinical records.

### Q13. What is actually needed (high level; detailed design is Phase 1)

New tables (all clinic-scoped, composite FKs, server-only where they hold clinical text):

- **Catalog/configuration:** lab test category, lab test (code, sample type, preparation text, turnaround, price, active), parameter (data type, unit), panel ↔ test membership, configurable reference ranges (dimensions limited to what patient data can support — see G1).
- **Workflow:** lab order (patient, ordering doctor, consultation appointment, `creation_key`), order item (test snapshot + price snapshot), sample, result + result values, result versions/verification.
- **Documents:** attachment metadata + new private bucket.

Extensions to existing objects:

- `payments`: subject generalisation (Q5).
- `notification_job_type` + job subject (Q8).
- `staff_role`: a lab role (Q7) — exact shape is a Phase 3 decision.
- `clinical-isolation.test.ts` FORBIDDEN list; `database.types.ts` regeneration.

New routes/UI: admin lab configuration, doctor ordering inside the patient
chart, lab work queue, patient Mini App results (subject to P2 below).

## 5. Gaps

- **G1 — Patient demographics.** `patients` has no date of birth, sex, passport or PINFL (`20260813000002:39-57`). Age/sex-dependent reference ranges (Phase 1/4) and safe import matching (Phase 13) both depend on them. Phone is indexed per clinic but is not an identity key.
- **G2 — No patient merge / duplicate detection** of any kind.
- **G3 — No Kassa UI, no receipts, status-only refunds** (Q5).
- **G4 — No document storage or signed delivery** (Q6).
- **G5 — Notifications are appointment-bound** (Q8).
- **G6 — One role per person per clinic** (Q7).
- **G7 — No Mini App deep-link routing** beyond booking (Q9).
- **G8 — Naming clash.** "Tahlillar" is already the admin nav label for **Analytics** (`src/app/admin/layout.tsx:34`) while "tahlil" also means lab test. The lab module needs a distinct label (e.g. "Laboratoriya").
- **G9 — Dashboard revenue inconsistency** (`dashboard/route.ts:54` uses catalog price).
- **G10 — No external lab integration of any kind**; no provider documentation in the repo (MedPlus is not referenced anywhere).

## 6. Risks and policy conflicts (blocking — need owner decisions)

The binding project rules in `AGENTS.md` currently say:

> Never show clinical text to operational staff, patients, logs, audit rows, analytics, or the patient-facing bot.
> AI must never write, read, or summarise clinical text.
> `clinical_records`: … laboratory orders and results … Readable only by doctors that `doctor_can_read_appointment()` admits.

Several later phases of the lab plan contradict these rules as written:

- **P1 — Lab staff (Phases 7–8).** Lab staff must see which tests were ordered and must write result values. They are not doctors. Either lab results are defined as a distinct category from doctor-authored clinical text with an explicit lab-staff access rule, or the work cannot proceed.
- **P2 — Patients (Phase 12).** Patients viewing their own results in the Mini App contradicts "never show clinical text to patients". This needs an explicit, owner-approved rule (which results, after which state, which fields), plus privacy-page wording (`TASKS.md` already lists privacy-wording reviews as open).
- **P3 — AI (Phase 18).** AI result/trend summaries contradict "AI must never … summarise clinical text". This phase must not start unless the rule is deliberately changed; until then the isolation test must keep blocking it.
- **P4 — Retention.** `clinical_records` (and every patient-linked table) cascade-deletes with the patient; `TASKS.md` lists "Clinical records legal review – confirm retention rules vs. the patient's deletion request" as open. Lab tables must follow whatever is decided; this audit proposes **no** retention period.
- **P5 — Reception/Kassa clinical leakage.** Billing a lab order shows test names to the cashier. Whether test names count as clinical text for reception must be decided (likely: catalog names are operational, results are clinical).
- **R1 — Two sources of truth** for labs (free-text `lab_order`/`lab_result` records vs structured data) — U2.
- **R2 — Payment subject change** touches booking-critical code paths (Q5); needs regression tests on the booking engine and Click webhook.
- **R3 — Generic audit trigger** (`audit_track_changes`) would copy result values into `audit_events` if attached to lab tables; lab tables need id-only audit triggers.

## 7. Migration concerns

- New enum values (`staff_role`, `notification_job_type`, any lab status enums extended later) each in their **own** migration; regenerate `supabase/full-db-setup.sql` with `npm run db:full-setup` and verify `--check`.
- New composite FKs on existing tables (payments) need the `20260930000006` pre-flight pattern so existing data cannot silently violate them.
- Making `payments.appointment_id` nullable changes an invariant relied on by `book_appointment` and the TS layer; must ship with booking-engine and payment regression tests.
- Do not use `on delete cascade` from `patients` into lab history until P4 is decided; follow the existing pattern (doctor FKs no-action) and document the choice.
- Regenerate `src/lib/supabase/database.types.ts` with each schema phase.
- Seeds: `supabase/seed.sql` is local-only; any lab demo catalog stays out of production (`scripts/seed-prod-catalog.sql` must not gain clinical reference ranges).
- Never apply migrations to production from these phases.

## 8. Proposed lab architecture (direction only)

```
clinics ─┬─ lab_test_categories
         ├─ lab_tests ── lab_test_parameters ── lab_reference_ranges
         ├─ lab_panels ── lab_panel_tests
         │
patients ┤
doctors ─┤
appointments (consultation) ──< lab_orders (ordering doctor's own appointment, creation_key)
                                   ├─< lab_order_items (test + price snapshot)
                                   │      └─< lab_samples
                                   │      └─< lab_results ─< lab_result_values
                                   │             └─ versions / verification
                                   ├─ payments (generalised subject)
                                   ├─ notification_jobs (lab subject)
                                   └─ lab_documents (private bucket)
clinical_records ← doctor's own interpretation, unchanged model
audit_events     ← id-only triggers + strict read audits
```

- Clinical-text tables (results, values, documents) are **server-only** like `clinical_records`; reads go through server routes that authorize via the existing access functions and audit strictly.
- Order lifecycle, sample lifecycle, result/verification lifecycle and payment status stay separate columns/tables.
- Reference ranges are configuration data; the app only reports "outside configured range", never an interpretation.

## 9. Unresolved architectural questions

- **U1** Lab grouping: lightweight category on the lab catalog (recommended) vs. reusing `specialties` (not recommended — public catalog and AI navigation exposure).
- **U2** Legacy `lab_order` / `lab_result` clinical record types: keep for manual/external entries, hide once structured data exists, or migrate? Must avoid double display in the chart.
- **U3** Payment subject model: nullable `appointment_id` + `lab_order_id` with exactly-one CHECK (recommended) vs. one payment per order item.
- **U4** Staff with more than one responsibility (doctor-verifier, reception-collector) under the one-role-per-clinic constraint: new roles only, or a capability table alongside `staff_roles`.
- **U5** Who may verify results (clinic-configured): which roles, and may the ordering doctor verify?
- **U6** Policy changes P1–P3 in `AGENTS.md` (lab staff, patient visibility, AI).
- **U7** Retention / deletion behaviour for lab data (P4).
- **U8** Patient demographics (DOB, sex, identifiers) — needed before configurable age/sex ranges and before import matching.
- **U9** Is a lab order always tied to a consultation, or can reception/lab register external or walk-in lab orders without a doctor visit?
- **U10** Ordering-time price: snapshot on the order item (recommended) so later catalog price changes never alter what was billed.

## 10. Recommendation

1. Build a **dedicated, server-only lab domain** that reuses patients, doctors, appointments-as-consultations, the existing clinical-access functions, the payment engine (with a generalised subject), audit, notifications and Telegram identity. Do not create a LabDepartment entity or reuse `specialties`; use a lab-catalog category.
2. **Before Phase 2**, have the owner decide U6 (amend `AGENTS.md` for lab staff, patient result visibility and, separately and later, AI) and U9. Without P1 settled, the database design cannot pick its RLS/grant shape.
